# Structure & Clarity Review

## Overall assessment

The manuscript has a strong, contribution-first structure and a readily traceable argument: distributed-information failure motivates evidence selection; the identical-state fork isolates the selector contrast; the results establish the within-protocol effect; and the Discussion separates intervention efficacy from routing. The principal scope qualifications are unusually explicit and generally comprehensible. The remaining issues are presentation-level rather than architectural, but several matter for a dense short-paper format: the title is broader than the operational construct, Table 1 is unnecessarily wide, a few analysis units are not explained at first use, and the final claim briefly exceeds the otherwise careful deployment boundary.

## Specific findings

### 1. Title and abstract

The title is memorable and foregrounds identification, but **“Evidence Direction Matters”** can be read as a general semantic claim. The experiment tests direction labels generated by the model under one selector bundle, not validated evidence direction. A title such as **“Model-Self-Labeled Evidence Direction Matters in Multi-Agent LLM Deliberation: An Identical-State Fork”** would align more closely with the manuscript's claim ceiling, though a shorter formulation may be preferable for presentation.

The Abstract successfully contains the problem, design, primary result, replication status, two sensitivities, and claim boundary. It is nevertheless very dense: nearly every sentence introduces a new qualification or numeric result. The last sentence, **“deciding truth-blind when to intervene,”** is awkward; **“deciding when to intervene without access to ground truth”** is clearer. Consider also replacing “the result survives” with the more literal “the contrasts remain negative” in the sensitivity sentence.

### 2. Logical flow and section transitions

The Introduction has a strong four-step progression: failure to pool information, the neglected evidence-selection decision, the same-state identification strategy, and three contributions. The Experimental Design then follows the order a reader needs: protocol, outcome, evidence qualification.

The final paragraph of Section 2.3 shifts from study qualification to artifact integrity. Because the blueprint assigns hashes and verifier boundaries to the supplement, this paragraph could be shortened to one sentence in the main paper and expanded in supplementary material. That would make the move into Results faster without removing the important distinction between internal consistency and provider provenance.

In Section 4, the exact-hash reuse statistic appears with little conceptual setup. Readers have not been told what “reuse” measures or why it was a routing candidate. Either define it in one short phrase or reduce the paragraph to the clearer high-level point: the tested truth-blind routing candidate did not replicate and is therefore excluded from the contribution claim.

### 3. Terminology and operational definitions

The manuscript consistently distinguishes the `ATTACKS` and `SUPPORTS` policies from validated semantics, and the distinction between internal pre-specification and external preregistration is clear. The separation between intervention efficacy and routing qualification is also one of the paper's clearest ideas.

However, first use should more directly follow the style profile's terminology: **“model-self-labeled `supports` or `attacks` evidence.”** That phrase would make the operational status visible before readers encounter the uppercase policy names. Likewise, “evidence direction” should be tied to model-generated labels whenever it could otherwise sound like an intrinsic property of the evidence.

The units in the missing-report analysis need one additional explanation. The denominator of 354 expected reports is not obvious from “90 task-seed blocks,” because tasks have different agent counts. A parenthetical such as **“one final report per agent, totaling 354 across the 90 blocks”** would remove this ambiguity. In Section 3.3, define “rescue” explicitly as a more negative ATTACKS-minus-CONTROL difference before using it as a noun.

### 4. Density and mathematical readability

The paper is compact without being skeletal, but Sections 2.3 and 4 carry many qualifications in rapid succession. Several are repeated across the Abstract, Methods, Discussion, and Conclusion. Repetition is defensible for the central claim boundary, yet secondary qualifications—manifest scope, the exact-hash detector, and the six-item limitations list—could be compressed to preserve emphasis on the causal contrast.

The Brier-score passage is conceptually clear, but the Markdown notation `p-bar_k = (1/M) sum_j p_jk` is visually inferior to the displayed equation that follows. Use consistent mathematical notation, for example `\bar p_k = M^{-1}\sum_j p_{jk}`, and define `M` and `K` at that point. This will matter in both PDF readability and oral/poster reuse.

### 5. Tables

Table 1 repeats each estimate and confidence interval in separate columns, producing seven columns. A four-column layout would be easier to scan and more likely to fit: **Contrast**, **Seed 0 estimate [95% CI]**, **Seed 1 estimate [95% CI]**, and **Two-seed task summary [95% CI]**. Its caption or table note should state that values are paired Brier-score differences and that negative values favor ATTACKS, so the table remains interpretable when viewed independently.

Table 2 is substantively effective, but the first-column labels are long and the two estimate columns are wide. Short row labels plus a caption defining tasks, blocks, and the complete-report restriction would improve visual economy. Boldface currently marks every negative estimate, even though all displayed estimates are negative; it adds little discrimination and could be reserved for the primary analysis or omitted.

### 6. Scope qualifications and conclusion

The scope qualifications are comprehensible and appropriately layered: the paper explains the custom protocol, single-model setting, post-hoc second seed, earlier task use, unvalidated target-free labels, unmatched exposure bundle, missing reports, evaluator-only severity, and replay boundary. Importantly, these qualifications do not obscure the positive within-protocol finding.

The main wording inconsistency is in the Conclusion: **“This establishes a usable intervention result”** sounds closer to deployment readiness than the Abstract and Discussion allow. Replace it with **“This identifies a substantial within-protocol intervention effect”** or similarly bounded language. Also, the claim that the complete-block check shows the primary direction “is not created by incomplete blocks” is slightly stronger than the immediately preceding admission that the missingness mechanism is unidentified. A clearer formulation is: **“the estimated direction remains negative when analysis is restricted to complete blocks.”**

## Recommendation

**Minor Revision.** The manuscript's organization, core terminology, and claim boundaries are already strong. Revision should focus on tightening the title and final claim, simplifying the tables, clarifying report/block denominators and the detector reference, and reducing secondary qualification density in the main narrative.
