## Manuscript Sections

### Introduction
Groups can collectively possess the information needed for a decision while failing to surface it in discussion. Human hidden-profile studies established that groups preferentially discuss shared evidence and can miss the best option~ [REF] . HiddenBench transfers this setting to multi-agent large language models (LLMs) and reports systematic failures under distributed information~ [REF] . Agreement alone is therefore not evidence of decision quality.
Work on multi-agent deliberation commonly changes whether agents debate, how messages are exchanged, or how final answers are aggregated~ [REF] . We isolate a smaller operational choice: after agents have already produced evidence, which subset should be made salient in the shared context? Resurfacing attack-labeled items might interrupt a developing error, whereas resurfacing support-labeled items might reinforce it. End-to-end comparisons cannot isolate that choice when the arms arrive with different earlier transcripts or beliefs.
We use an identical-state fork. Within each task and seed, round~1 is executed once. CONTROL, SUPPORTS, and ATTACKS inherit the same in-memory round-1 objects and the same recorded transcript, beliefs, evidence registry, and state hash. They use the same runner, adapter, model configuration, provider seed, round-2 procedure, and final elicitation; only the disclosure selector and resulting continuation prompt differ. Final private probability reports are scored after the run closes. The design estimates a finite, within-protocol contrast from the same realized pre-treatment states.
Contribution. Identical-state forks identify a causal effect of model-labeled evidence selection on collective proper loss, and both selector-contrast directions replicate under a second model family. A content-matched GLM component experiment then narrows the mechanism claim: selected-item re-exposure relative to control reproduces across two recorded seeds, while the explicit ATTACKS-frame increment does not. DeepSeek seed~0 remains the project-internal confirmatory main experiment; GLM provides replication and bounded component evidence. Cross-domain generality, salience-versus-reasoning mediation, and truth-blind allocation remain open.
Operational scope. Evidence selection is not an ancillary formatting choice. Any bounded-context multi-agent system that must choose among more generated content than it can surface implements an exposure policy, explicitly or implicitly. Our results identify it as a causal control surface: changing which already-produced evidence receives shared attention changes final proper loss across two model families. The applicable setting has separate or private reports, competing options or hypotheses, retainable evidence, a post-disclosure re-judgment stage, and later external calibration. Candidate mappings include multi-agent question answering, event assessment, diagnostic assistance, code and security review, and analytic decision support; none is empiricall

### Experimental Design


### Tasks, agents, and protocol
We use the 45-task treatment-evaluation split from a pinned 65-task HiddenBench snapshot~ [REF] . Task descriptions, options, private facts, and answer keys are reused as data; our two-round protocol is not a reproduction of HiddenBench's reported protocol. Each private fact defines one agent, yielding four agents for most tasks and three for tasks 30, 34, and 50. The DeepSeek runs use deepseek-chat , temperature~0, and a provider seed derived from task and fork seed.
Round~1 is simultaneous. Each agent receives shared task information and its private fact, then returns a categorical probability vector and natural-language evidence items self-labeled supports or attacks . Agents do not see one another's round-1 output before responding. Their messages enter a common transcript, while structured evidence items enter a registry with content hashes.
After round~1, every task-seed state is continued under all three policies: itemize CONTROL : disclose no structured registry items; SUPPORTS : disclose registry items self-labeled supports , deduplicated by content hash; ATTACKS : disclose registry items self-labeled attacks , deduplicated by content hash. itemize
Each arm receives the common round-1 transcript. Structured disclosure can therefore resurface content already expressed in that transcript; salience, rather than information novelty, is a plausible explanation. Round~2 is simultaneous, followed by a private final probability elicitation whose responses do not re-enter discussion.
The labels are operational model outputs, not oracle-validated relations. The schema does not require a target option, so an attacks item may challenge any option, including the correct one. Exposure amount and source count were not matched. Across 90 DeepSeek task-seed blocks, SUPPORTS disclosed means of 5.344 items, 1,691 characters, and 3.678 source agents; ATTACKS disclosed 5.489 items, 1,741 characters, and 3.456 sources. The estimand is therefore the implemented selector bundle, not an isolated semantic property of evidence direction.

### Common estimand and model-specific executions
The two model families are studied as separately frozen executions of one common estimand, not as a single pooled experiment and not as an exact model-name-only substitution. The shared scientific object is the same: after the realized round-1 state, the choice among CONTROL, SUPPORTS, and ATTACKS changes final collective proper loss, evaluated by task-paired differences of pooled multiclass Brier with task-level bootstrap intervals.
The executions differ in scope and in completion discipline. DeepSeek used 45 tasks, three arms, two seeds, and lenient valid-report pooling. GLM-4.6V used 44 tasks---the same treatment-evaluation roster with task~14 excluded, because task~14 had been observed in earlier mechanism work---and a stricter fail-closed completion policy under which any expected agent report that fails qualification in round~1, round~2, or the final elicitation marks the whole task failed, with no retry or replacement. The GLM executions also used a different provider adapter, parser normalization (code-fence stripping), token caps of 768/256, and thinking disabled. The two-arm GLM seeds (0 and 1) ran CONTROL and ATTACKS; the three-arm GLM seed (2) ran CONTROL, SUPPORTS, and ATTACKS. These are conceptual and directional replications: they share the task family, arm definitions, outcome, and paired analysis, but they are not identical protocols across providers, and effect magnitudes are not claimed to be invariant across models.

If an arm has no valid final report, its Brier is null and that task-seed block is unavailable for contrasts involving the arm. The run performs no analysis-time repair or retry. Answer keys are read only offline; selectors receive no correctAnswer , groundTruth , or later resolver output.
For contrast $A-B$, we compute the paired arm difference within task $t$ and seed $s$. DeepSeek seed~0 estimates the equal-task mean over its 45 realized task states. Each GLM seed is a single seed with one block per task; the three-arm seed~2 additionally supplies the ATTACKS-minus-SUPPORTS contrast that the two-arm seeds cannot. These are finite-sample protocol estimands, not estimates of a declared benchmark or model population.
Confidence intervals use 10,000 percentile task-bootstrap resamples generated by mulberry32 with master seed 0x5EED0F . In two-seed analyses, seed effects remain grouped inside their resampled task. Leave-one-task-out checks remove each task in turn.

### Evidence qualification
H1 (ATTACKS minus SUPPORTS) was primary and H2 (ATTACKS minus CONTROL) secondary. For DeepSeek, the seed-0 direction, equal-task analysis, and decision rule---an interval wholly below zero---were frozen in the repository before treatment outcomes were inspected. This was project-internal prospective specification, not an externally timestamped preregistration. We make no joint familywise-error claim across H1 and H2. DeepSeek seed~1 and all pooled or restricted analyses were added after seed-0 inspection and serve as robustness evidence.
The GLM executions form a separately frozen replication ladder. GLM seed~0 was a prospective two-arm replication whose plan and analysis specification were frozen after the DeepSeek results and before any GLM provider call. GLM seed~1 was a random repetition whose standalone decision rule was frozen after GLM seed~0 was inspected. GLM seed~2 was a full three-arm extension frozen after the two-arm results, reproducing the entire ATTACKS/SUPPORTS/CONTROL conceptual contrast. Each execution was analyzed with its own frozen specification and replay-verified artifacts; they were never pooled into one pre-registered estimate.
The 45 DeepSeek tasks were held out from inspection of ATTACKS/SUPPORTS fork outcomes during development. Eighteen had nevertheless appeared in earlier experiments involving different measurements or interventions. Such prior use defeats a claim that the set was broadly unseen, but it does not reveal the same-state fork potential outcomes. We retain the experiment and directly report a sensitivity excluding all 18 tasks. The GLM population inherits the same roster minus task~14, whose earlier observation motivated its exclusion.
Recorded DeepSeek artifacts contain 270 arm rows (45 tasks $ $ 2 seeds $ $ 3 arms). Within each task-seed block, the arms share the recorded round-1 state hash. GLM artifacts record per-attempt request hashes, provider request identifiers, token usage, and whole-task qualification failures. Replay recomputes recorded Brier values and verifies manifest-listed files. This is an internal-consistency boundary, not proof of provider-side provenance or of files outside the manifest; historical DeepSeek raw provider responses were not retained.

### Content-matched component probe
After inspecting the selector replications, we separately froze a GLM-4.6V component experiment on the 44-task roster. Each task generates one new round-1 state and forks it into CONTROL, ATTACKS\_NEUTRAL , and ATTACKS\_LABELED . The treatment arms receive the same attack-selected items in the same order with identical source IDs, hashes, and observation text; their prompt-visible relation frame differs. M1 is labeled minus neutral, M2 neutral minus control, and M3 labeled minus control. The runner uses independent deserialized state copies, a shared selection-identity commitment, rotating arm order, strict whole-task completion, and offline-only scoring. Seed~3 is the first frozen component execution. Seed~4 followed seed-3 inspection; its later rate-limit recovery enters only post-hoc sensitivity and never replaces original Batch-B failures.

### Qualification and robustness
Excluding all 18 broadly prior-used DeepSeek tasks leaves 27 tasks and preserves both effects (Table~ [REF] ). Restricting to task-seed blocks with every expected final report leaves 41 tasks and 76 blocks and also preserves both directions. The latter restriction is selected using a post-treatment variable and cannot establish that missingness is ignorable.
The actual three- and four-agent rosters imply 354 expected final reports per arm in DeepSeek. Valid counts were 331/354 for CONTROL (93.5\%), 347/354 for SUPPORTS (98.0\%), and 345/354 for ATTACKS (97.5\%). Only task~60, seed~1, CONTROL had zero valid reports and therefore a null arm Brier. Since multiclass Brier lies in $[0,2]$, imputing that outcome at either bound gives pooled H2 means from $-0.314$ to $-0.336$; both endpoint bootstrap intervals remain below zero (upper limits $-0.164$ and $-0.195$). This bounded check protects the H2 sign against that null arm but does not explain differential invalid reports elsewhere. GLM handles missingness differently---whole-task fail-closed exclusion with worst-case $ 2$ bounds---and those bounds also remain negative (Section~3.2).

### When does the effect emerge? Belief-revision trajectories
To localize the effect across revision stages, we recomputed pooled round-1, round-2, and final beliefs and independently re-derived Brier at each stage (frozen in FIRST\_PAPER\_BELIEF\_TRAJECTORY\_ANALYSIS\_FREEZE\_V1.md , corrected under V2; recomputed final Brier matches stored values to floating-point precision; round~1 is a hash-verified shared start). This is a post-hoc mechanism diagnostic, not a preregistered mechanism test.
Q1: When does the effect emerge? Figure~ [REF] shows task-level mean Brier at round~1, round~2, and final. In DeepSeek, the ATTACKS-CONTROL separation already appears at round~2 ($-0.298$ $[-0.406,-0.199]$) and is nearly unchanged at final ($-0.330$ $[-0.497,-0.188]$); ATTACKS-SUPPORTS is $-0.352$ $[-0.483,-0.233]$ at round~2 and widens slightly to $-0.412$ $[-0.571,-0.267]$ at final. In the GLM three-arm seed, ATTACKS-CONTROL is $-0.576$ $[-0.708,-0.447]$ at round~2 and $-0.602$ $[-0.773,-0.436]$ at final; ATTACKS-SUPPORTS is $-0.399$ $[-0.564,-0.239]$ at round~2 and $-0.386$ $[-0.590,-0.192]$ at final. The treatment effect therefore emerges immediately after disclosure at round~2 in both model families; the final elicitation mostly preserves, rather than creates, the separation.
Q2: Does ATTACKS correct confident errors? On the offline, post-hoc subset of round-1-top-choice-wrong blocks (60 DeepSeek blocks across 32 tasks; 29 blocks/tasks in the GLM three-arm seed), task-equal ATTACKS-CONTROL raises final correct-option probability (DeepSeek $+0.308$; GLM $+0.478$) and lowers the original wrong top choice's probability ($-0.160$; $-0.340$), consistent with correction rather than only belief flattening. The subset uses the answer key and cannot be used online; switch counts and the rescue balance are in the supplement (S22.3).
Q3: Does group dynamics change? In both families, ATTACKS shows higher round-2 alignment, lower max pairwise total variation, higher evidence reuse (DeepSeek $+0.368$; GLM $+0.290$), and more pooled top-choice changes from round~1 to final. These descriptive dynamics are not evidence of ``deeper reasoning''; full values are in the supplement (S22.4).
Interpretation boundary. Immediate post-disclosure separation with high evidence reuse is consistent with salience or resurfacing; pooled top-choice and disagreement changes are compatible with reasoning reorganization, but do not establish it. The content-matched probe below separates the explicit relation frame from selected-content re-exposure. Neither experiment observes whether re-exposure acts through attention, reasoning reorganization, or both.

Negative values in Table~ [REF] favor the first named arm. Neutral re-exposure (M2) and the full labeled treatment (M3) remain negative across executions.

### Exploratory targeted-rescue heterogeneity
An offline post-hoc analysis groups DeepSeek tasks by pooled round-1 Brier, which requires the answer key and is unavailable online. On 15 catastrophic tasks with round-1 Brier above 1.0, ATTACKS minus CONTROL was $-0.79$; on 28 moderate tasks it was $-0.11$; on the two tasks below 0.3 it was $+0.01$. Round-1 Brier correlated with ATTACKS-minus-CONTROL rescue (Spearman $ =-0.60$, bootstrap 95\% interval $[-0.80,-0.34]$). The GLM seed-1 severity gradient is directionally consistent but its interval includes zero. The pattern is consistent with targeted rescue, but post-hoc thresholding, room to improve, and regression to the mean remain alternative explanations; two low-severity tasks cannot support a non-harm claim. ### Discussion and Related Work
The experiment shows that structured evidence selection after the same recorded state changes collective probabilistic decisions, and that both selector-contrast directions replicate under a second model family. The component probe makes the interpretation narrower: under GLM-4.6V, resurfacing the attack-selected bundle helps relative to control without requiring an explicit ATTACKS frame. It does not show that every selected item is valuable; harmful task-level effects occur. Public attention is a testable treatment variable, while the model-generated label remains an unvalidated selection input.
DeepSeek provides the same-state causal identification: within each task and seed, only the disclosure selector differs after the realized round-1 state. GLM-4.6V substantially weakens the alternative that the effect is a single-model or single-provider coincidence: the ATTACKS-CONTROL direction is negative in all five model-seed implementations, and the full three-arm seed reproduces the ATTACKS-versus-SUPPORTS contrast as well. Within the replication ladder, the two GLM two-arm seeds provide random-realization replication (task-effect Spearman 0.770 across their 38 common tasks), while the three-arm seed provides the conceptual-contrast replication. The GLM effect magnitudes are larger than the DeepSeek ones, but because the executions differ in adapter, parser, completion policy, population, and arm set, no model-only attribution is possible; the replicated object is direction, not magnitude.
The result complements multi-agent debate~ [REF] and recent work on when debate helps and how confidence or response diversity relate to outcomes~ [REF] . Our design holds the realized pre-treatment state fixed and changes which already-produced items are made salient before continuation. It also differs from work diagnosing conformity and error propagation~ [REF] , because our primary endpoint is evaluator-side proper loss caused by a post-state intervention.
Intervention efficacy does not solve routing. No truth-blind trigger is qualified, and the oracle severity analysis cannot be deployed online. The component result suggests that a future allocator should decide when to resurface selected content rather than rely on an explicit ATTACKS instruction, but that policy is untested. Detector construction and social thermodynamics belong to the second paper.
Limitations. All executions share one HiddenBench task family, and cross-model protocols differ beyond model name. Relation labels remain target-free and unvalidated. Fixed arm order leaves provider drift collinear with treatment in the original selector runs; the component probe rotates orders but cannot eliminate drift, and its neutral/labeled frames differ in wording and length rather than one token. Seed~4 followed seed-3 inspection, and its recovery sensitivity was acquired in a later provider/time/credential batch; it cannot be promoted to primary completion or prove missing-at-random. ### Conclusion
From the same recorded round-1 states, model-label-conditioned disclosure changed final proper loss under DeepSeek, and both selector-contrast directions replicated under GLM-4.6V. A later content-matched GLM probe showed that selected-item re-exposure, not a reproducible incremental ATTACKS frame, is the stable tested component across its two recorded seeds. This supports a scoped exposure-policy claim---not universal evidence semantics, a model-independent mechanism, or a deployment rule. Truth-blind allocation of re-exposure should be tested independently.
